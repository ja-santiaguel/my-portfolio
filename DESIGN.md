# Design — Jonathan Andrew portfolio

The design source of truth: what we're aiming for, what Jonathan likes and dislikes, where the inspiration comes from, every design decision made so far, and the UX/UI standards everything is checked against.

**Read this before any visual work** (layout, type, colour, imagery, motion, components). **Update it** whenever a design decision is made or Jonathan reacts to a visual: add the reaction to the taste log with the date, in Jonathan's words where possible.

---

## Before building anything visual

1. **Taste:** read the likes, dislikes and on-hold ideas below. Does the idea repeat a dislike? Does it build on a like?
2. **Decisions:** check the system and page decisions for the area you're touching; stay consistent unless the change is the point.
3. **Standards:** check the UX/UI standards section. Taste never overrides accessibility or usability.
4. **Motion:** anything that moves also goes through the `motion` skill (`.claude/skills/motion/SKILL.md`).
5. **Ask first** for major changes (brainstorm, agree, then build), and treat Jonathan's questions as questions, not go-aheads.
6. **Afterwards:** log Jonathan's reaction in the taste log, and new decisions in the decisions sections.

## Ambition

**An Awwwards-nominee-level site** (2026-10-11): an experience that feels unparalleled because of craft, not volume. Every page has its own clear visual expression and a point of view (Jonathan loves how each Awwwards site is unique), but effects never get in the way of using the site.

- Test every effect: *does this help the visitor understand or enjoy the work?* If it slows reading, hides content or exists only to impress, cut it or tone it down.
- One memorable moment per view; everything around it quiet and precise.
- The work is the hero. The site frames it.
- **The 55-second rule** (2026-10-12): reviewers decide in about a minute, so every page proves role, quality and outcome at a glance, and shows the work live and in motion rather than explaining it. Depth stays available for interviews, one click away. The site's own craft is part of the proof (visual polish and craft is the top criterion hiring managers cite).
- **AI-assisted build** (2026-10-12): this portfolio is built with AI assistance (Claude Code); say so where the build is described (colophon, the portfolio's own case study).

**Feel:** brutalist in the architectural sense (honest structure; mass in the type and the work), confident and **precise: lines are fine, never heavy** (since 2026-10-12, after The Shift); fluid, fast and reactive; cool monochrome so the work stands out.

**Story:** architecture shapes the structure (grids, title blocks, drawing-set thinking). The kitchen background gets a brief mention only (constant testing and adjusting, discipline).

## Design system

The rules every visual implementation starts from, extracted from what already works on the site.

### Colour
- Concrete page `--background` #f3f3f3, ink `--foreground` #151515, darker concrete for panels and mats `--panel` #e6e6e3.
- Muted grey `--ink-muted` #6b6b6b (4.8:1 on concrete) for secondary meta only, never on interactive elements. Inside difference-blended chrome use `--ink-muted-blend` #888 (renders as #6b6b6b on concrete).
- The work brings the colour.

### Type
- Every text style uses the role tokens in `src/styles/globals.css` (`--type-<role>-size/-weight/-tracking`), never raw values.
- Roles: display-xl (hero), display-l (case-study/About titles), display-m (list titles, "Interested in…"), display-s (case-study section titles), lead, body, prose (case-study reading text: every block of case-study copy, In short, Context, decisions' Client ask / Why / Trade-off and Lessons, in one size, 2026-10-19, after Jonathan spotted decisions at 14px beside Context at 18px; the same day lowered to the body size, 14px / 1.6, because the larger reading size is kept for the project description beside the title, the lead at 20px, on the case study and the landing page: "the 18px size should only be used sparingly"), heading (case-study sub-headings), statement (a fact to stop on, large in the text face: `clamp(1.75rem, 3.3vw, 3rem)`, regular, -0.015em, leading 1.12; 2026-10-19, Aether’s problem, after Jonathan asked to accentuate it as an important fact), label, label-s (cursor block), chrome (navbar + back-to-top credit), meta.
- Faces: Oswald for display (700 and 600; Oswald has no 800, so 800 renders 700), Helvetica Neue for everything else (600 labels, 400 the rest, Heavy 800 only for "[ View … ]" calls to action and bold lead-ins in case-study text).
- Tracking in em: 0 display, 0.01em lead, 0.02em body, 0.06em labels, 0.08em chrome (12px needs more air), 0.1em meta.
- Stacked lines of mixed sizes: `text-box: trim-both cap alphabetic` plus an explicit gap, so gaps measure equal (byline).
- Not yet tokenised: the old Aether case-study body type (waits for its rewrite).

### Grid and layout
- **8 columns**, `--grid-gap` 1rem between them, `--grid-margin` 4rem outer margins on desktop, `--grid-margin-sm` 1.25rem below 1024px. Write grids as `repeat(8, minmax(0, 1fr))` so long words never push columns wider.
- Column spans in use, and what they're for:

```
cols    1    2    3    4    5    6    7    8
        ├────┼────┼────┼────┼────┼────┼────┤
slide        [ text 2–5   ][ media 5–8    ]     home list slide
footer       [ content 2–8                 ]    contact ending, next project
case    [toc][ reading 36rem   ][ margin   ]    case study (own 3-track grid)
bento   [ featured 2×2 ][ tall ][ square   ]    grid view (4 tracks, 2 rows)
```

- **Column 1 holds the case-study content rail**, never content. Content starts in column 2, the navbar's time column, at every width.
- Case studies use the same 8 columns so every edge lines up with the navbar (2026-10-13; **main content confined to columns 2–7 since 2026-10-18**):
  - **Columns 1 and 8:** navigation aids and flair only (the back link and the content rail in 1; 8 is kept open), like the home page's index strip.
  - **Columns 2–7:** the title, lead, facts and all scrolling content; reading text 2–5; margin notes 6–7; wide screens 2–7.
  - **Full width (allowed to break the columns):** the 3D laptop, the ink showcase band, the footer, the navbar, and section-breaking full-width visuals.
  - **Facts strip:** Role 2, Team 3, Timeline 4, Tools 5, **Status** 6–7 (2026-10-16): labelled and top-aligned like the others, its value then the "[ View live site ]" link under it; no longer a right-aligned aside.
  - **Blocks of three** ("In short", context facts, lessons): two columns each, 2–3, 4–5, 6–7.
  - **Showcase:** one screen across 2–7, then a staggered 3 + 3 pair; the process stage 2–5 with its steps in 6–7.
  - **Process steps:** one column each across 2–5 (two each below 1280px).
  - **Next project:** from column 2.
  - **Allowed to break the grid:** deliberate flair only (the full-width recording; optical title offsets), and the footer's centred email-and-socials trio, for visual balance (2026-10-16).
- **Two-up below 1024px** (2026-10-23, after "things that line up should line up"): every two-column arrangement on phones and tablets (the case-study facts, the footer's LinkedIn and Instagram, any future pair) uses the grid's gutter and starts its second column exactly on page column 5. They had drifted to three different x positions.
- **Reading measure:** long text is never wider than `--reading-measure` (36rem ≈ 65–70 characters at 18px).
- **Subgrid** for components that must line up with the page grid (case-study sections, figures with margin captions).
- Fixed chrome sits on the margins: navbar top; back-to-top bottom-left; project index on the right edge and List/Grid bottom-right while the work section is in view.

### Spacing
One scale, in rem (tokens in `globals.css`):

| Token | Value | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| `--space-3xs` | 0.25 | stacked label lines, label to value |
| `--space-2xs` | 0.5 | icon to text, small groups |
| `--space-xs` | 0.75 | label above content, caption gap |
| `--space-s` | 1 | grid gap, gaps between plates |
| `--space-m` | 1.25 | paragraph spacing, phone margin |
| `--space-l` | 1.5 | inside small panels |
| `--space-xl` | 2 | inside panels, title to text |
| `--space-2xl` | 2.5 | around figures |
| `--space-3xl` | 3 | between groups inside a section |
| `--space-4xl` | 4 | desktop margin, larger group breaks |
| `--space-5xl` | 5 | sub-section breaks on phones |
| `--space-section` | 7 | between sections |
| `--space-section-l` | 9 | page top under the navbar, major breaks |

Rule: things that belong together sit closer than things that don't (proximity), and each step up the hierarchy takes a bigger step on the scale. New work uses the tokens; older components still carry the same values as raw numbers and migrate when touched.

### Breakpoints
- **Responsive pass (2026-10-16):** checked every page at 375, 768, 1024, 1280/1440 and 1920. Below 1024px the navbar uses the compact layout (no clock), the corner label hides and List/Grid moves into the flow at the top of the work section; 1024–1279px hides the clock, scales slide titles (`clamp(4.5rem, 7vw, 6rem)`) and gives the case-study facts strip two rows. The legacy pages (Aether, Arasaka, Reckless; About until its 2026-10-27 rebuild) get a shared layer in `globals.css` (`[data-legacy]`): one column, mobile margins, scaled titles, no fixed section title, left-aligned text. `RiseText` now re-measures its lines from its block container, so line breaks follow the real width. (The `[data-legacy]` layer was removed 2026-10-28: every page now uses the new layouts.)
- **< 640px** phone: one column, spec labels above values, pairs stack.
- **< 768px** small tablet: the decorative back-to-top corner hides.
- **< 1024px** tablet: grids collapse to one column, sticky elements become static, `--grid-margin-sm` margins.
- **< 1280px** narrow desktop: margin captions move under their images.
- **≥ 1471 × 956** large: display titles step up (list titles to 8rem).
- Layouts must work at every width from 375px to 1440px+, not just at the breakpoints.

### Surfaces and depth
- **Concrete** (`--background`) is the page. **Ink blocks** (`--foreground` fills) are for the few elements that must sit on top of anything (the cursor label, inverted hover tabs). **No grey mats, no grey banners, no frames** (disliked 2026-10-12): screens sit straight on the page; summary blocks (facts strip, "In short", Full story) open with a **fine 1px ink rule**. Small labels that sit on screens (Before/After, Drag, Pause/Play) are **concrete tabs** like the bento labels, not black blocks. `--panel` is reserved for media that hasn't loaded yet.
- **No rounded corners, no soft shadows, no gradients.** Depth comes from layering and contrast. The one exception is the cursor label's faint 1px concrete edge, so it holds its shape over dark media.
- **Fine lines for precision:** dividers are 1px in `--line` (ink at 16%: subtle separators); markers that show state (the content-list marker) are 1px ink. The mass lives in the type and the work, not the lines. Keyboard focus outlines stay 2px so they're easy to see.
- Where lines appear: case-study summary blocks, the home slides' spec tables (title-block rules between rows, drawing in from the left with the slide's entrance), and above the footer's name / get-in-touch row.
- **Stacking order** (low to high): page content and the hero title words (0) → hero side text (3) → the flying hero photo (4) → slide titles (1001) → fixed chrome: back-to-top (1000), navbar (1001), List/Grid (1002) → cursor (top).

### Motion vocabulary
What already moves, so new motion reuses it instead of inventing more (tokens and rules: the `motion` skill):
- **Clip reveal** (signature): home media open from a slightly inset clip as they scroll in. Not on case-study media (2026-10-15), where true sizing matters more.
- **Loop videos:** short screen recordings that show the real product working; pausable, on-screen only.
- **Depth parallax with a lagged follow:** project media drifts slower than the page.
- **Hero flight:** the portrait travels into the first project with a lagged move and resize.
- **Split-flap:** the hero title flips between words.
- **Text entrance** (2026-10-18, after mikewilcockdesign.com's wordmark; replaced the rise out of a mask everywhere): two animations per line or letter. **The rise** moves it up on `--ease-rise`, an underdamped spring that passes its mark by ~4% halfway and settles softly back ("the subtle and soft overshoot and correction"); display letters start half a letter-height low (0.45em, shortened from 0.9em on 2026-10-18) and ~5% wide, narrowing as they land (measured from his site: ~1.1 s, letters ~120 ms apart; his has no positional overshoot, the narrowing and long settle read as one, and ours adds the small overshoot Jonathan described); body lines start a full em low (2026-10-19, was 0.35em, too short to feel the curve). **The focus** clears opacity and blur (`blur(0.1em)`, scaling with the type) in the first part of the travel, so the blur only shows while the text is low (Jonathan: "make the blur apply when the letters are lower"). **Timing (2026-10-19, after Mike Wilcock's text):** lines rise, unblur (0.08em) and fade together on `--ease-reveal` over `--dur-reveal` (1.1 s), 50 ms apart, on load and on trigger alike; display letters keep the overshooting `--ease-rise` (1.1 s, 35 ms apart, focus `--dur-short`), the one place the overshoot Jonathan asked for stays. Nothing is clipped.
- **Entrances on trigger, not tied to scroll** (2026-10-19; replaced the scroll-driven rise of 2026-10-13): Jonathan, "the timings are more consistent and fluid instead of sometimes being too fast if a user scrolls too fast". One site-wide observer (`ViewTrigger.js`) marks any `[data-trigger]` element `[data-in]` once its top passes 12% above the bottom of the screen, and CSS plays a fixed-length entrance from there. It un-marks only when the element leaves through the bottom (scrolled back up above it), so the entrance replays coming back into view but never while scrolling on past. Text still rises **per laid-out line** (`RiseText` measures the real line breaks) and display titles letter by letter (`RiseLettersScroll`); section and summary rules draw in on the same trigger; the case-study content rail clip-opens item by item (50 ms apart) while the story is on screen and closes in reverse. Content is hidden ahead of its entrance only once the observer runs (`html[data-trigger-ready]`), so it never stays invisible without JS. Reduced motion: no entrances. Above the fold (hero side texts, case-study header) lines still rise on load, 50 ms apart. **Still scroll-driven by design:** the footer's column cover (its text went trigger-based too, 2026-10-19), the 3D laptop sequence, home clip reveals and parallax.
- **Exits a little sooner** (2026-10-19): scrolling back up, content leaves once its top drops below a line 8% of the screen above the entry line (about 80% down instead of 88%), if it had risen above it; otherwise at the entry line. Coming back it re-enters at the line it left by, so it never flips. Display letters rise one by one but **leave together**, the whole title at once.
- **Process stage step** (2026-10-19): worked out from the steps' positions on every scroll (the last whose top has passed the middle), not remembered from the last one to cross it, which went stale on a fast scroll and flashed Build.
- **Scroll-driven motion holds under the page transition** (2026-10-19, Jonathan: the footer columns and the home media clip "instantly snapping to complete" when a link was clicked mid-way): the transition no longer sets `overflow: hidden` on the page (it took scroll-driven animations off their timeline, so they fell back to finished); scroll input is blocked by PageTransition itself (wheel, touch, keys, Lenis), and whatever was mid-way stays exactly where it was under the cover.
- **Entrances wait for the page transition** (2026-10-19): while `html[data-page-transition]` is set, triggered entrances queue and play once the cover lifts, and load-mode text and the header rule hold; arriving by "Back to work" or the navbar, the landing page’s lines used to draw in behind the cover.
- **No flicker** (2026-10-19): ViewTrigger judges exits by the element's resting position (its own transform taken out), because a screen starting its entrance 3rem low dropped back below the trigger line and flipped between entering and leaving every frame.
- **Every fine line draws in on a trigger** (2026-10-19, Jonathan: "make all fine lines load in triggered and not scroll controlled"): the home slides’ detail-row rules (label part, then the value part 90 ms later, on the reveal curve; drawn back when scrolled up) joined the case-study section rules and the footer rule. The header’s details rule draws with the load sequence. Still scroll-driven, by design and not lines: the footer’s ink columns and the home media clip reveal.
- **Ink fill** (2026-10-19, `InkText`; Jonathan asked for it on Aether’s problem instead of the rise): text waits in faint grey (`--ink-faint`, ink at 20%), already readable, and darkens to ink word by word on trigger (35 ms apart, `--dur-medium`, the smooth spring; two statements fill as one sentence); scrolling back up it plays in reverse, the last word greying first (20 ms apart, `--dur-short`, `--ease-exit`; Jonathan, 2026-10-19). For facts worth stopping on, not body text. **Aether’s context is scroll-tracked** (Jonathan, 2026-10-19): `mode="scroll"` ties each word to the block’s pass through the screen (inked over 12–48% of its cover range, the sentence complete by the middle), un-inking as you scroll back; both lines share one timeline. Taken from joshgrafik.com’s grey-to-ink reveal, played on a trigger rather than tied to scroll.

- **Media entrance** (2026-10-19, matching the text): case-study screens (decision screens, process stage, before/after, showcase panels and phones) rise 3rem and fade on `--ease-reveal` over `--dur-reveal` (2026-10-19, Mike Wilcock's images: 48px, 1.1 s; the 10px blur was dropped, as a full repaint of a large image every frame for little visible gain), on the same trigger (`[data-media][data-trigger]`); a pair staggers 120 ms (`--d`). No clip-open on case-study media (true sizing first, 2026-10-15).

- **Focus close-in:** on case-study screens, a darkening closes in from the frame onto the feature a note describes (in answer to hover, focus or tap).

Clicks during a switch are ignored. **The clip reveal travels with the frame** (2026-10-20, Jonathan: "the snapping to full clip mask ... when I'm in between project sections", and grid-to-list images "not respecting the clip mask effect that should be in-place given their position"): each frame's window animates its clip from where it was to where it will rest; a list frame's clip is worked out from its position (the same rule as the CSS reveal: 14% top and 5% sides at the bottom edge, open once 80% has entered), because a freshly rendered frame's scroll-driven clip resolves a frame late; grid cells are open. ## UX/UI standards

Checked before calling visual work done (the `ship-check` skill runs the measurable ones).

**Accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA)**
- Text contrast ≥ 4.5:1 (≥ 3:1 for large text and UI parts); check blended text visually.
- Visible keyboard focus on everything interactive; logical tab order; no keyboard traps; a scrollable region must be focusable.
- Visible label words appear in the accessible name ("label in name"); icon-only controls have an `aria-label`.
- Targets ≥ 24px (2.2 minimum), aim for 44px on touch.
- One `h1` per page, no skipped heading levels, landmarks, `lang` set, descriptive alt text (decorative images `alt=""`).
- Motion: respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; nothing flashes more than 3 times a second; no scroll-jacking without a fallback; anything that moves on its own for more than 5 seconds has a pause control (2.2.2).
- Colour is never the only signal (pair grey with weight, position or wording).

**Reading and type**
- Long-form text 16–18px, 45–75 characters per line, line height 1.5–1.7, left-aligned.
- Body copy never below 14px; small uppercase labels need extra tracking.
- Headings and captions should tell the story on their own for skimmers.

**Interaction**
- **Touch targets (2026-10-16):** on touch screens every control is at least 44px tall without moving anything, via `.touch-target` (globals) or per-component padding with a matching negative margin. Links wrap their rise masks (masks clip vertically and would cut the target back). Exceptions, all above the 24px WCAG minimum and never overlapping: navbar Work and About me (~27px, grown away from each other) and the navbar icons (32×44).
- Feedback within 100 ms; hover effects mirrored on `:focus-visible`; touch devices never depend on hover.
- Nielsen's heuristics as the sanity check: visible system status, real-world language, user control (undo, back, skip), consistency, error prevention, recognition over recall, minimalist design, clear errors.
- Proximity: controls sit near what they control; conventions are broken only on purpose, with a reason.
- Links look like links or carry an affordance (brackets, arrow); external links say they open a new tab.

**Layout and responsive**
- Works from 375px to 1440px+ with no horizontal scroll; content order makes sense when stacked.
- Fixed elements never cover content that the visitor needs to read or tap.

**Performance**
- First Load JS ≤ 150 kB for `/` and case studies; total first-load transfer ≤ ~1.5 MB; images optimised (WebP/AVIF via `next/image`, correct `sizes`).
- Animate only `transform`, `opacity` and `clip-path`; no layout shift during animations.
